# The Paladin's Revelation — World Bible

*Companion to the character bible. Continent: Aethon. Era: roughly medieval Europe — 13th to 14th century feel.*

---

## Aethon at a glance

A continent of forested hills, river valleys, an old salt-eaten coastline in the west, and a long mountain spine in the east. Climate temperate. Cities are stone-walled, towns timber-and-thatch, villages mud and beam. Roads exist between major settlements and degrade fast in wilderness. Travel by horse is the privilege of the noble or the desperate. Most people walk, work, and die within a day's ride of where they were born.

**Tech level:** medieval-Europe-adjacent. Plate armor for the wealthy; mail and gambeson otherwise. Crossbows are common among soldiers, longbows among hunters and rangers. **No gunpowder.** Steel is good but not abundant. Books exist but are expensive. Most people are illiterate; clergy and nobility read.

**Time:** Aethon, in some form, has stood for centuries. Civilization stalls in ways no one can name. The lore answer — the secret war between vampires and dragons keeping humanity from advancing — is hidden from the reader.

---

## Religion: the cult of the Light

The dominant faith of Aethon. Worships *the Light* as a divine, life-giving force. The Hallowed Order is the Light's militant arm. Lesser clergy run village chapels and minister to common folk.

**What the Light is publicly:** divine grace, the source of paladin power, the holy fire that purifies sin. Iconography: sun-rays, golden flames, halos, candles.

**What the Light secretly is:** dragon fire, mythologized. The vampires founded the cult to militarize humanity against dragons while keeping dragon magic flowing through the Order's relics. Nearly every "ray of light" in iconography, looked at sideways, is a stylized flame or fang. Stained glass and altar carvings hide dragon imagery in their own ornament.

**For the prose:** Aric should walk past these all his life and never see them. The reader catches glimpses he doesn't. A candle that burns the wrong color in chapter 1. A stained-glass window where the rays are too curved, too clawed. He admires it. The reader gets a quiet shiver.

## The Hallowed Order

**Public structure:**

- **Grand Master** — public head of the Order. Currently *Hallam Voryn*, an aged and devout man who genuinely believes everything the Order teaches. He is unaware of the vampires. He may not survive the series.
- **Chancellor** — the Grand Master's chief advisor and political arm. **Chancellor Valen.** The real power.
- **Grand Inquisitor** — head of internal investigations, heresy, and dragon-related matters. **Maldren.** Public enforcer. Vampire.
- **Knight-Captains** — regional commanders who govern districts and answer to the Hallow.
- **Paladins** — full knights of the Order. Take the single oath. Carry relics or relic-touched arms. Aric is one.
- **Squires** — paladins-in-training, typically aged 14 to 20. Ride with their masters.
- **Lay brothers and sisters** — serve the Order without taking the oath. Stewards, scribes, smiths, cooks, healers.

**Hidden structure:**

The vampires (Valen, Maldren, and an unknown number of others) are not part of the formal hierarchy in the way they appear to be. They are older than the Order itself. They wear its titles like a costume. When Valen "reports to" the Grand Master, it is a performance for an audience that does not know it is performing.

**Headquarters: the High Sanctum** — a vast cathedral-fortress of pale stone in the capital. Aric has been there twice. His knighting was there.

**Aric's home base: the Chapel of Light** — a regional outpost in the eastern Marches. Stone, old, smaller than its name suggests. Houses fewer than twenty paladins and lay brothers. From here, paladins ride out on missions assigned by knight-captains who take their orders from the Hallow. The Aldric seat is a half-day's ride west; Mirren made the journey to see Aric off.

---

## Other knightly orders (background only)

- **The Wardens of the Sundered Coast** — older, weakened. Once guarded the western coast against threats now mostly forgotten. A few keep their oaths in crumbling sea-fortresses.
- **The Order of the Bastion** — even older. Most chapter houses ruined. A handful of elderly knights still wear the device.
- **The Free Lances** — not an order but a category. Knights without affiliation, mercenary and proud. Some are former paladins who quietly walked away — a fact the Hallowed Order does not advertise.

These exist mainly so Aethon feels populated. None play roles in book 1 beyond mention.

## Geography of Aethon

**Major regions:**

- **The Hallow** — central highlands. The capital city (also called *the Hallow*) sits at its heart, with the High Sanctum at its center. Political and religious heart of Aethon.
- **The Marches** — southern and western midlands. Agricultural, rolling. The **Aldric seat** is here; the **Eldric holding** is a half-day's ride away. The **Chapel of Light** sits in the eastern Marches, on the road that runs east toward the Reach.
- **The Reach** — eastern frontier. Rough country where the foothills of the Spine begin. Dragon attacks happen here, when they happen. Sera's village is in the Reach.
- **The Spine of Aethon** — the great mountain range running the eastern border. **Mount Khaer'gul** is one of the highest peaks. Ancient. Dragons nest in the upper crags.
- **The Northwood** — vast forest north of the Marches. Rangers live here. **Doran**, Edmund's old friend, is one of them; he meets Aric on the Old Eastroad in chapter 4. Ravens nest thick in the Northwood, which makes the prose work for chapter 7's spy-raven moment.
- **The Sundered Coast** — western seaboard. Old, salt-eaten, half-mythic. Reserved for later books.

**Aric's route in book 1:**

1. Chapel of Light (eastern Marches) — chapters 1–2
2. East along the **Old Eastroad** through the Northwood — chapters 2–4 (the ranger encounter is on a forest road)
3. A crossroads in the Northwood — Ysolde appears here, chapter 5
4. Sera's village in the Reach — chapter 6
5. Through the Reach foothills — chapters 7–9
6. Up into the Spine, Mount Khaer'gul — chapters 10–14
7. Riding back west under a new dawn — chapter 15

Roughly two to three weeks of riding from chapel to mountain. Long enough to ride into a different self.

## Magic in Aethon

Magic exists in this world. **Humans cannot cast it.** There are no schools of sorcery, no mortal mages, no spells learned and channeled at will. The continent has no wizards — only soldiers and priests and scribes.

But that is not the same as saying magic does not touch human lives.

**The four sources of real magic in Aethon:**

- *Dragons* — fire, ferocity, the instinct to guard treasure. Dragons were once human. The curse stole them.
- *Vampires* — immortality, the drinking of life, the binding of minor demons (the ravens), the art of being subtly forgotten. Valen and Maldren wear humanity like a coat.
- *Angels* — extremely rare. Sera/Seraphina is one. Her gifts are quiet: faster healing in her presence, fevers that break a day early, wounds that mend cleaner than they should. The divine has been pushed to the margins of this world by the long secret war.
- *Relics* — dragon-touched objects whose magic seeps into anyone in long proximity. The five great relics of the series, and lesser dragon-influenced things scattered through Aethon.

---

### Paladin "blessings" — the great cruel joke of the Order

Paladins do experience things that resemble magic. Wounds close faster than they should. Strength comes when they pray under pressure. Clarity descends in combat that approaches premonition. Senior paladins, in their later years, have been seen to call faint flame to their hands during great battles. The Order teaches that this is *the Light's favor* — granted in proportion to a paladin's piety. Pray more, and the Light blesses you more. Sit longer in the cathedral and feel the warmth grow. Devote yourself, and you will know power.

It is all the dragon curse.

**Relic proximity is the actual mechanism.** Major relics are kept in the great churches of the Hallowed Order — chief among them the High Sanctum. The most devout paladins, those who pray longest in those holy spaces, are sitting closest to dragon-magic for the most hours of their lives. The curse seeps into them. Their "blessings" deepen. So does their corruption. They cannot tell the two apart, because the Order has not given them the language to.

The Order has, through the centuries, built a system in which **its most pious knights are also its most doomed.** They are the ones who eventually have visions they cannot explain, dreams of fire, sleepless nights, the wrongness in the body that no priest can name. By the time anyone notices, the Order has already arranged a "final crusade" from which they never return.

Edmund Aldric was one of these. He carried two relics and prayed at the High Sanctum for years. His "blessings" were larger than most paladins ever feel. He was a celebrated hero of the Order. Then he was sent away.

---

### Aric in book 1

He is junior. He carries Dawnbreaker, one major relic. His prayer life has been at the regional Chapel of Light, not the High Sanctum, so his curse is in early stages. What he experiences:

- The pommel of Dawnbreaker runs warm in his palm when he grips it hard. He thinks the blade is *answering*.
- His grief over Linnet sits oddly clear in him — not numbed, but contained. He thinks this is the Light's strength.
- He sleeps less than he should and wakes alert. He thinks this is a paladin's discipline.
- His dreams, increasingly, glow.

None of this is dramatic enough to be obviously supernatural. The reader catches the texture; Aric does not.

Once he leaves the Chapel of Light in chapter 1, he is no longer near the chapel's small relic-cluster, and the curse's progression slows. Dawnbreaker alone keeps it alive. This is why he can ride for weeks and still seem mostly himself when he reaches Mount Khaer'gul. He is being seasoned, not consumed.

Chapter 11's recognition includes, secretly, a recognition of *this* too: that the heat in his hand has been there all along, and it was never the Light.

## The ravens

Bound minor demons, cursed to inhabit raven-shape on Aethon and die into the cycle again and again. Bound to vampire service. They serve as eyes and messengers for Valen and Maldren.

**In book 1:**
- Chapter 3: a single raven appears in flashback, premonition of Edmund's last crusade
- Chapter 7: Aric notices ravens following him on the road — first attributed to coincidence, then to his own disquiet, then (correctly) to the truth
- Chapter 15: a lone raven on a branch as Aric rides toward dawn — foreshadowing book 2

The reader should not learn what the ravens are in book 1. The truth is reserved.

---

## Mount Khaer'gul

A bald grey peak in the northern Spine. Snow above the treeline most of the year. The summit ridge holds caves carved by older dragons, mostly empty now. **Vaeltharion's lair is a high cirque just below the peak**, accessible by a single switchback path that crossbow-armed soldiers can fortify into a kill-zone.

This is the geography of chapter 10's ambush: soldiers wait in concealed positions along the switchback while Valen sends Aric ahead, alone, to draw the dragon's fire. The reveal is staged so that Aric — riding into what he believes is honor — is in fact riding into a designed killing-ground intended to take the dragon and the paladin together.

## Calendar, currency, daily life

**Calendar:** twelve English months (January through December), familiar to the reader. Year reckoning by the founding of the Hallowed Order — *the year 312 of the Hallow*, or similar. Aric's quest takes place across late summer into early autumn (the cold of the Spine matters in chapters 10–14).

**Currency:** silver crowns, bronze pennies. A loaf of bread is a penny. A horse is several crowns. A paladin's relic-blade is priceless.

**Common folk life:** small holdings, subsistence farming, market days. Most villages have a chapel and a Light-priest. People fear dragons in the abstract more than they fear the Order. They are wrong to.
